the problem

You decided to start meditating.
Then you opened the app store.

Five hundred guided journeys. Seven-day challenges. Sleep stories, soundscapes, subscriptions. A thousand doors, and every one of them says begin here.

We have never had more tools for calm.

And we have never felt more disconnected. Most people don't fail at meditation. They fail at choosing how to start.

my instinct

I teach meditation the pragmatic way.

Start a timer. Count your breaths. That's it.

No incense, no astral planes, no library of five hundred sessions. It works for the people I sit with. But is that what the world wants — or just what I believe?

the research

So instead of guessing,

what came back

The market wasn't asking for another library of calm.

the way

The data didn't give me a product.
It gave me permission.

First Breath is meditation with everything removed except the part that works. No accounts of enlightenment. No five-hundred-session library. No guru.

One timer. Counted breaths. Nothing else.

Try it now.

That's the whole product.

This is what go-to-market looks like when you stop guessing —
and let the web itself show you the way.
